# Keeneland Race 12, Sat Oct 31 2026

The Grade 1 Breeders' Cup Classic, 1 1/4 miles on the dirt for 3-year-olds and up, purse $7M, Beyer par 113. Eight entered, seven runners after Magnitude scratched. Post 6:16 PM ET. Odds are morning line (DRF Formulator, 9 AM).

## Off the list

**Forever Young (2-1)** is the defending champ and the most accomplished horse in the race (16 starts, 12 wins, $32.3M; won this race last year with a 111). He came back with a 105 winning the JC Gold Cup at Belmont off six months away, so he should be tighter today. No price, though.

**Sovereignty (5-2)** owns the top figure line among the elders (115 in the Travers last year, 112 winning the Whitney in August) and is 3-for-3 at 10 furlongs. The question is the foot abscess that kept him out of the Gold Cup; reports say he has trained well. Also no price.

**Scratched:** Magnitude (6-1, Asmussen/Prat), who beat Forever Young in the Dubai World Cup and beat Sovereignty in the Stephen Foster. His exit takes out a major player and a stalker, and it improves every price below.

No probable scratches beyond that.

## Pace

Two horses want the lead: Knightsbridge (TimeformUS early pace 126) and Napoleon Solo (125). Forever Young (100) sits right behind them and DRF's notes expect both favorites to go after the speed early. That sets up a hot, contested pace for 10 furlongs. The best late numbers belong to Golden Tempo (127), Sovereignty (125), The Puma (125) and T O Password (120).

## Playable horses, ranked

**1. Golden Tempo (5-1)** is the play. Best per-start money among the playable horses by a mile ($791,667 a start this year on 6 starts, $4.75M; 2025 was one maiden win), and the trend has gone one way all year: 81, 84, 88, 95, 98, 100. He won the Kentucky Derby (95) and the Belmont (98) and closed for 3rd in the Travers (100) after cutting the corner, beating The Puma in that race. He is 4-2-0-2 at a route of ground and 7-for-7 in the money on dirt. Ortiz (22%) and DeVaux (17%) are 24% together ($2.12) and 19% at Keeneland ($2.36), and DeVaux hits 26% on dirt ($2.52). He has the best late pace in the field, and two 125+ speed horses plus Forever Young pressing them is the exact race a closer needs. The risk: his 100 is 13 points under par and 11 under the two favorites' best, so if the pace is honest without being fast, he is giving away a lot of ground and figure. 3-year-olds have won a third of the Classics.

**2. Knightsbridge (9-2)** has the top figure in the race, a 122 drawing off in the Monmouth Cup in July and a 112 in the Gulfstream Mile, both at or above par. He handled 10 furlongs winning the Pacific Classic (104) by 5 1/2 lengths after shaking off a duel. Per-start is $205,602 this year (4 wins in 6). Mott is 15% and Saez 16%, 27% together ($1.91), and it says something that Mott runs him even with Sovereignty in the race. The risks are real: the 122 came against a soft field, the Pacific Classic figure was a 104, and Napoleon Solo will be on him from the gate with Forever Young next to them. He may also be bet near 7-2.

**3. The Puma (10-1)** is the improver with a price. Per-start $160,326 on 7 starts, and the line rises 94, 99, 103, the 103 winning the Pennsylvania Derby G1 off a big 6-wide move. Castellano (16%) and Delgado (11%) are 25% together ($2.00). Late pace 125. He has never faced elders and Golden Tempo beat him in the Travers, but at 10-1 he is the best flyer.

**4. Napoleon Solo (15-1)** is rising too (96, 99, 103 in the Travers, beaten a half-length on the lead) with big per-start money ($338,304 this year, a Preakness win). He is the main speed and that is the problem: Knightsbridge and Forever Young will not let him walk. Summers is 4% off 61 to 180 days.

**5. T O Password (30-1)** is a good Japanese dirt horse (5 wins in 12, Timeform 106 when 3rd in the Elm S.) whose only US figure is a 90 in the 2024 Derby. Per-start about $65,000 this year. Too slow on what we can measure.

## Bottom line

Golden Tempo at 5-1 is the bet: the most money per start, a figure line that has risen every race, the best late kick in a race with too much speed, and a hot Ortiz/DeVaux combo. Knightsbridge at 9-2 is the second choice on raw figures if he survives the early heat (pass him if he drops under 7-2). The Puma at 10-1 is the flyer. Watch the board: Golden Tempo could take money as the 3-year-old with the Derby/Belmont double.
