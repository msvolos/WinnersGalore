Pace

Plenty of speed. War Strategy (early 113), Arbiter (111), Victory Achieved (108) and Ready to Collect (105) all want to be up close, with Nobals (101) right behind them. My Boy Prince (late 89) is the only real closer. That is a tiebreaker only: speed has held more often than it has collapsed in the tracked races.

The contenders

Victory Achieved (2-1), the horse most likely to win. His last start was a career-best 100 winning the Grade 2 Nearctic on good turf at Woodbine, edging away from Nobals by 2 1/4. Before that he ran 92, 91 and 92 in three straight stakes seconds and a GP allowance win, so the line is steady to rising and the last figure is already over the 98 par. He is 4-for-9 on synthetic. Hernandez 21%, Casse 20%, and the pair are 31% together at Woodbine with a $2.13 ROI. Six weeks between starts suits a Casse runner. The risk: he is coming off a 9-point career top, he carries 124, and War Strategy and Arbiter both have more early speed, so he may have to sit in a contested pace. About 30%; fair odds 2.3-1, so 2-1 is a slight underlay (0.90).

Arbiter (3-1), the best figure on today's surface and distance. The lone 3-year-old ran a 98 (exactly par) winning the Woodstock at this track and trip by 5 3/4 in April, "when asked had plenty." He is 2-for-3 on synthetic and 2-for-2 at the distance. The one bad line is a 70 on CD turf in June, his only try on grass this year, which I throw out as a surface experiment. The question is five months off; he has steady Keeneland works and Ward is 26% with 61-180 day layoffs. Moran 20% at the meet and the combo is 44% at Woodbine (9 starts). Lightly raced, 20%+ connections, the profile that has hit the board most. Facing older stakes horses for the first time at a 4-pound break. About 22%; fair 3.5-1, and 3-1 gives 0.88.

My Boy Prince (5-2), the class of the field on paper. Life earnings over $1.6 million, Beyers of 101 and 100 in the spring, and a perfect-looking 7-5-2-0 synthetic record at Woodbine. The trouble is the trend: 93, 92, 88 and 92 since, with "gave way" and "shuffled" in the last two, and he has never run 6 furlongs (the distance line shows no starts). He is the only closer in a speed-heavy field, which helps, but I will not lean on the pace. Aebly 19%, Casse 20%, J/T 21%. About 18%; fair 4.6-1, and 5-2 is a big underlay (0.63).

Nobals (6-1), the defending champion. He won this race last year with a 95 and has run 87, 66, 90, 92 and 86 this year, three of them thirds and one a clear 2nd to Victory Achieved in the Nearctic. 7-for-14 on synthetic and 4-for-9 at the distance. Rodriguez 16%, Rivelli 25%, J/T 34% overall. The knock is that he is 7, 0-for-5 this year, and his best this year is 92. About 13%; fair 6.7-1, and 6-1 gives 0.91.

G T Five Hundred (8-1), a 6-for-9 synthetic horse who won the Grade 2 Connaught Cup with a 95 in July. He bled when last in the King Edward in August and has been away three months since. Douglas 13%, Desruisseaux 17%, J/T 20%. About 8%; fair 11.5-1, and 8-1 gives 0.72.

Bottom line

There is no overlay on the morning line. The board's 26% take shows in every price. Victory Achieved is the top pick (best value ratio among the likely winners, last-out figure over par, 31% connections at the track) and becomes a bet at 16-5 or better. Arbiter is the second choice and a bet at about 9-2 or better. Nobals is a hair higher on value ratio (0.91 vs 0.88) but at a far lower win chance, 0-for-5 this year, so I used the close-call tiebreak (higher win chance) for the second spot; he is a bet at 9-1 or better. War Strategy is the flyer at 10-1. If Victory Achieved is 2-1 or shorter at post and Arbiter drifts to 9-2, bet Arbiter.
