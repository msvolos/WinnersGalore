What happened

The handicap expected a speed fight: Guapo, Legacy Account, Connors Outlaw and Lil Elvis all wanted the lead, which looked like a setup for horses sitting off the pace. The fractions were quick but not crazy for a 6-furlong stakes, and one of those speed horses, Connors Outlaw, held on to win at about 23-1. The closer we liked as the second pick, Missin Hollywood, finished last.

Grading the handicap

The top pick was right about the horse and wrong about the race. Easy as Pie ran to his rising form and beat everyone but the winner, which is exactly what a place and show ticket is for. The pace read missed: the race did not fall apart, and the winner came from the group we expected to tire. Connors Outlaw's per-start earnings ($2,486 this year) were the weakest of the contenders, so the earnings screen never had him on the radar.

Takeaway

A crowded speed map at 6 furlongs does not guarantee a collapse. At short sprint distances, speed often holds, so a closer should not get a big upgrade on pace projection alone.

Playable horses, ranked

1. Easy as Pie (9-2) - the pick. Second-best per start this year ($15,435) on a small sample, backed by a solid $10,590 last year. The trend is the sell: Beyers went 59, 49, 52, 63 as a young horse, then jumped to 81 in a game second at Remington in August and he followed it with a 67 win last out going 7 furlongs (chased 3 wide, dueled, edged clear as the favorite). He is 3 for 4 at Remington. Hamilton only wins at 8%, but Biehler is 15% at the meet and the pair together hit 31% with a $3.31 ROI here, which is a live combo. He has tactical early speed (93) without needing the lead. The risk is the class jump from a first-level allowance into a stakes against older Oklahoma-bred sprinters, and he has to repeat or top that 81.

2. Missin Hollywood (8-1) - second choice. Per start $10,824 this year, steady figures in the mid to high 70s (78, 57 with a hop at the start that I throw out, 77 winning a $50K Oklahoma-bred stakes in March, 74 second on turf last out). He is a closer (late pace 77) in a race full of front runners, so the pace should come back to him. Ashford is 23% on the year. The knock is that he is 6, Luzzi is only 9% at the meet, and this J/T team is 0 for 15 at Remington.

3. Breakable Code (7-2) - class of the field, short price. Won this exact race last year and earned $28,720 per start in 2025. Only two starts this year (66 in a sprint where he lunged at the start, 77 rallying third on turf last out), so I lean on last year. Cabrera is the leading rider here at 22%. He is the most likely winner, but at 7-2 he is the minimum price and probably goes lower, so there is little value.

4. Mi Saturday (20-1) - the flyer. Career-best 86 and a 85 winning a $50K stakes in February. Last out was a turf sprint (52, 4 wide, no threat), a surface experiment I throw out. Before that he ran 72, 69 and 67 in decent spots. Works since August are sharp. De La Cruz (18%) won three times on him. At 20-1 there is a lot of price for a horse whose top figure matches anyone's here.

5. Guapo (10-1) - fastest last out (86, won a $25K claimer by 9+ going 5 furlongs) but it is a big jump in class, he stretches to 6, and he has to deal with Legacy Account and Connors Outlaw for the lead. The barn is 2% in stakes. Bounce candidate.

6. How About Bob (8-1) - honest 3-year-old with a good late kick (84), runs 64 to 75. Huckaby is 2% and Swan is 0 for 25 at the meet. Hard to trust the connections.

7. Slades Tank (6-1) - beat Cajun Stoops in a $30K claimer last out but the figures have slipped (72, 72, 68, 62). Silva is hot at 25% but the horse is going the wrong way.

8. Lil Elvis (12-1) - just broke his maiden; 3-year-old into an open stakes. Good barn, needs a jump.

9. Cajun Stoops (10-1) - flat form; broke in the air last time. Strong J/T ROI ($5.16) but the numbers do not stack up.

10. Connors Outlaw (5-1) - 6-year-old in decline ($2,486 per start this year), new trainer is 0 for 14. Overbet at 5-1.

11. Legacy Account (20-1) - more early speed in a race already full of it.

Bottom line

Easy as Pie at 9-2 is the play: rising form, strong record over the track, and a live jockey/trainer combo. Missin Hollywood at 8-1 is the second choice and the one to use if the early pace is as hot as it looks. Breakable Code is the horse to beat but likely too short. Mi Saturday at 20-1 is the small flyer. Check the board at post since Easy as Pie could drift either way.
