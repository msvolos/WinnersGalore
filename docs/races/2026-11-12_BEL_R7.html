The contenders

Nobody in this field has run within 10 points of the 89 par. The best recent figures are 79s from Stand the Rain and Consensus View, so this is a soft N1X where a mid-70s horse with a decent trip can win.

Stand the Rain (5-2) is the most likely winner. Her line reads 77, 77, 70, 79, and the 79 came last out winning a maiden at 1 5/16 miles going away from an inside trip. She is proven at a route this long (4 starts at today's distance, a 2nd and a 3rd, plus the win at 1 5/16) and Franco (22%) with Clement (17%) are 29% together at Belmont. The knocks: she is a maiden winner facing winners for the first time, she carries 126 (top weight by two), and she lost to Consensus View in June. About 26%; fair 2.8-1, and 5-2 gives 0.91. Underlay at the morning line, playable at 4-1.

Consensus View (3-1) beat Stand the Rain with a 79 in that June maiden, then ran 75 in the Saratoga Oaks (G2) and 76 in this same N1X condition at Saratoga, tracking and one-paced both times. Throw out the 64 on Oct 11 (race came off the turf, brushed). Prat (23%) and Chad Brown (25%) are 31% together at Belmont. She has not moved forward since her maiden win, and the closing style (late 110) needs pace that this race may not have. About 20%; fair 4-1, and 3-1 gives 0.80. Underlay.

Bobby M's Girl (12-1) is the value. She ran 75 last out in the $200k Ticonderoga (6th after bobbling and getting stuck inside behind tiring horses) and 73 in a 105k N1X before that, both off a ten-month layoff, so third start back she should be at her sharpest. Her life best is 77, which puts her inside two points of the top figures here, and she was 3rd and 4th in N1X turf routes at Aqueduct and Belmont as a 3-year-old. Castellano (18%) rides for Duggan (13%), 2-for-4 together at Belmont. The risk is plain: she is a deep closer (late 105, early 57) in a race with one real speed horse, a 5-year-old still trying to clear this level, and her N1X tries have come up short, so she needs the pace to cooperate. About 11%; fair 8.1-1, and 12-1 gives 1.43. Overlay.

Toes (7-2) is the only real speed (early 89, nobody else above 76) and could get a soft lead. She ran 77 at Santa Anita and 76 winning a maiden, then stumbled at the start and was 8th first back from ten months on Nov 1 (excused). The connections are the problem: Drysdale is 13% at the meet and 5% with allowance horses, and this jockey/trainer pair is 6% over 16 starts, under the 10% floor. A lone-speed upset is possible. About 15%; fair 5.7-1, and 7-2 gives 0.68. Underlay.
