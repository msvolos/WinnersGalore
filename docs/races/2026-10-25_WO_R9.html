Race recap

Result: Ciunas won ($6.10, $3.80, $3.10), Souped Up 2nd, Ready to Collect 3rd. Overskate Stakes ($150,000), 7 1/2 furlongs on a firm inner turf course in 1:28.40 (:21.91, :44.83, 1:09.73, 1:21.96). Exacta 2-1 paid $35.40 ($1) and the trifecta 2-1-8 paid $40.97 (20-cent). No scratches.

What happened

The pace call was right. Paramount Prince went to the early lead, Hunt Master forced it from outside, and the two dueled through :21.91 and :44.83 before Paramount Prince edged clear on the far turn. Ready to Collect broke well, then tucked in behind the leading pair and stalked from outside a rival. He took the lead past the quarter pole but lacked the late kick.

Ciunas saved ground near the back along the inside, moved off the rail on the far turn, found room four wide in upper stretch and struck the lead past the eighth pole, drawing clear at about 2-1. Souped Up tracked inside and came four to five wide for second.

Rapid Test exchanged a bump after the start, settled at the rear on the outside and was forced eight wide turning for home. He ran on and just missed third. He went off at about 3-1, so he was below the 7-2 line and would not have qualified at post time. Paramount Prince weakened to 5th and Hunt Master gave way to last.

Grading the handicap

The shape of the race was called correctly: too much speed, and the closers came. The write-up picked the wrong closer. Ciunas was dismissed at 9-5 as no value, with a 95 late pace and the comment that he "stormed home" last out, and that is exactly how he won. Rapid Test's eight-wide trip cost him ground, but he also got bet down to about 3-1, so the bet would not have stood. Ready to Collect ran a solid race at his first try past 7 furlongs and paid a small show profit, about what the risk notes predicted.

Takeaway

After two straight races where the projected pace collapse did not happen, this one did, and a short-priced closer we had dismissed got the setup. When the pace read points to a favorite who closes, the play is often a pass, and Rapid Test's late money at the windows said the same.

Pace

There is a lot of speed. Ready to Collect (Early 113), Paramount Prince (110), Souped Up (110) and Hunt Master (105) all want to be on or near the lead. The best late pace numbers belong to Rapid Test (Late 100), Ciunas (95) and Next Pitch (88). A contested early pace at 7 1/2 furlongs sets up for a horse finishing from mid-pack.

Playable horses, ranked

1. Rapid Test (6-1), top pick. He won this exact race last year (Overskate, 7 1/2 on the turf, 88 Beyer, "bid 1/8, edged past" over Hunt Master) and is 4-1-2-0 at today's trip with a 90 top figure ($80,830). The 2026 per-start of $12.6k undersells him: he is 0-2-3 in six starts this year, mostly chasing Paramount Prince in route stakes on synthetic, and last year's $30.1k on two starts is closer to his level here. Form is steady and solid: 88, 88, 86, 82, 89, the 89 a closing 3rd in the Grand Valley on turf at 9-1 behind the two favorites. He also ran a closing 2nd going 7 1/2 on the turf in June ("closed well last 16th"). Best late pace in the field, and the race shape suits him. Connections are the weak spot: Adams wins 6% at the meet, Day-Phillips 14%, J/T 13% with a $2.06 ROI. The risk is that he hits the board and does not win; he has not won since this race a year ago.

2. Ready to Collect (10-1), second choice. Best per-start among the playable horses in both years ($23.9k and $22.3k) and 7-for-15 on the Woodbine turf. Rising: 80, 86, 83, 87, the 87 a clear win at 7 furlongs on the turf last out ("keen to go, drew clear"). Roger Broomes is 2-for-8 at the meet and the J/T is 2-for-3 ($4.57). The questions are real: first try past 7 furlongs, a jump from starter allowance to a $150k stakes, and he is the fastest early horse in a race full of speed. If he gets loose on the lead he can go a long way at a big price.

3. Hunt Master (15-1), flyer. The course-and-distance horse: 9-4-2-1 at 7 1/2 on the turf, and he missed this race by a neck last year at 20-1 (87). I am throwing out his July line at Delaware (dirt route, tired) and treating the 75 last out as a prep at 6 furlongs, short of his best trip. His spring turf miles at Houston were 91 and 88. Bullet 5f work (1:00.2, 1/16) on Oct 11. He is 7 and the trend is down, and he needs the lead in a race where three others want it.

4. Dark Screen (8-1). Steady 88/84/84/89 and 7-2-2-0 on the turf, but he is a 5-furlong turf sprinter and his one try at 7 1/2 produced a 76 and a one-paced 5th. Winless in seven this year; Crawford 9% and Chubb 6% at the meet. Pass at the stretch-out.

5. Souped Up (10-1). A sharp synthetic sprinter (won the Bold Venture G3 at 90) who is 0-for-2 on turf with a top figure of 66. Faded in the Vigil last out. Pass.

6. Next Pitch (20-1). Top Beyer 80, still eligible for a N3L allowance. Outclassed.

Bottom line

Rapid Test at 6-1 is the play: the defending winner, proven at the trip, and the best closer in a race with too much speed. Ready to Collect at 10-1 is the second choice on rising form and the best earnings profile among the prices. Hunt Master at 15-1 is a small flyer for the course-and-distance angle. Check the board at post: if Rapid Test is bet below 7-2 he falls off the list.
