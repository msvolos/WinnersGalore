What happened

The handicap expected three speed horses (Duration, Freedom's Echo and Bartlett) to burn each other up and hand the race to a closer. The pace never got hot: :46.97 for the half and 1:11.49 for six furlongs is moderate for a one-turn mile stakes. With no collapse, the class of the race won. The 5-2 favorite Wild Vine, the other late-pace horse, won easily. Bartlett, one of the speed horses, kept going for 3rd, and Duration, who wanted the lead, faded to 5th.

Grading the handicap

The write-up said it plainly: Wild Vine was the most likely winner and no value at 5-2. That was correct. Margie's Fun Son needed a fast pace to have a shot, and he did not get one, though 4th behind the favorite was a fair effort. Bartlett, named as a saver, ran 3rd.

Takeaway

Third time in four races the projected pace meltdown did not happen. Before backing a closer on pace, check how fast the speed horses have actually gone early in their recent races, not just how many of them there are.

The only real late-pace horses are Margie's Fun Son (late 110) and Wild Vine (late 100). A contested pace over a one-turn mile sets up a closer.

Playable horses, ranked

1. Margie's Fun Son (6-1). The diamond. His per-start number nearly tripled from 2025 ($5,496) to 2026 ($15,079), and he has won 3 of 8 this year. His key line is the Mayor's Mile here on Aug 18, at today's exact distance: he trailed, circled the field and won by 2 1/2 lengths with a 90, beating Wild Vine. I'm throwing out the last line (74, trailed, no bid) because that was the $300k Dirt Mile against a much stronger field, and he still beat one home. His Beyers in that stretch (87, 82, 90) are on par with this group except Wild Vine. He has 16 starts at the distance (3-2-5) and he is 5-for-13 at Parx. Abrams wins at 18%, and the trainer angles fit a price: Routes 18% ($2.76), Dirt $2.25, Stakes 1-for-3 ($11.67), and the J/T line is 2-for-7 at a $6.06 ROI. The risk: Castillo wins at only 10%, and the regular rider (Martinez) is off. As a deep closer, he needs the speed to collapse, and if Wild Vine gets first run, Wild Vine is the faster horse.

2. Duration (5-1). He has the best recent figure among the playable horses: a 96 when he wire-jumped an OC 50k at 6 1/2 furlongs last out. His per-start number climbed ($11,892 to $21,553), and he is 4-for-6 this year. Pino is a 30% trainer with Sprint/Route at 39% ($1.88) and 2Off45-180 at 33% ($2.08), both of which apply today. The knocks: he is 0-for-5 at the distance (2 seconds, 2 thirds). He lost the rider Sanchez to Wild Vine, and Rodriguez is 1-for-13 at the meet. He also has to outrun Freedom's Echo and Bartlett early. If he gets loose on the lead, he is the upset threat.

3. Bartlett (9-2). He has the best per-start number among the playable horses ($27,719, steady on $26,333) and is 5-for-9 at Parx. Throw out his last start: he was trying to come back on 6 1/2 furlongs and took up at the 3/8. Before that he won two stakes and ran a 94 in a $100k allowance. He also fits Servis's Sprint/Route angle (24%, $1.75). The concerns: he carries 126, the barn is cold at the meet (8%), the J/T combo returns only $0.97 at Parx, and he is another speed horse in a crowded pace.

4. Dreambuilder (7-2). He sits right on the line. He is 3-for-6 at the distance and won his last start handily (82), but his per-start numbers ($7,608 and $10,449) are the lowest in the field. His figures top out at 82 to 86, short of these. He is moving up from an OC 50k after 55 days off. J/T 26% at Parx is the plus. Pass.

Bottom line

Play Margie's Fun Son at 6-1 to win, place and show. He has already beaten the favorite at this trip, and the pace should come back to him. Duration is the second choice and the horse to fear if he gets an easy lead. Bartlett is a saver if the board drifts him to 6-1 or higher. Wild Vine is the most likely winner and is no value at 5-2.
