What happened

The two best horses on figures and form finished 1-2. Magical Trickster, who beat Signature Winner on debut, did it again. The 6-5 favorite Souper Survivor, Signature Winner's stablemate, settled for 3rd. Signature Winner never threatened.

Grading the handicap

The second pick's case was the right one: best figure among the playable horses (67), a win over the top pick already on the record, and jockey Moran choosing to ride him over Signature Winner. The write-up flagged the top pick's real problem, a top Beyer of 62 against 73 for the favorite, and that gap decided the race.

Note the price: Magical Trickster paid $6.20, meaning he went off near 2-1. That is under the 3.5-to-1 rule, so on the live board he would have been off the list. The tracker grades on the morning line, so this win counts here, but it would not have been a bet at post time.

Takeaway

When the top pick's best figure trails the contenders by 10 points or more, the "proven at the distance" angle is not enough. A jockey leaving a horse to ride a rival is a tell worth weighting.

Off the list on the odds rule

No probable scratches. Everyone's last line is weeks back, and the PPs show no cross-entries.

Playable horses, ranked

Signature Winner (6-1). Top pick. Per-start about $20,300 on two starts (no 2025 record; all six are 2-year-olds, so this year is the only sample and it is small). He is the only horse in here who has already won at today's trip: 7f on the Woodbine synthetic on Oct 1, asked by the quarter and clear the rest of the way, "safe" at the wire. The Beyer dipped from 62 to 50 in that win, but he did it on his own and was never asked hard, so I read that as a race won with something left more than a horse going backward. His other line was a 2nd beaten 1 1/2 by Magical Trickster after dueling into the turn. Connections are the best fit on the board: Tharrenos wins 23% overall, 29% with 2-year-olds at a $3.87 ROI, and this jockey/trainer combo is 27% at a $4.87 ROI. He shares the barn with the favorite, which keeps his price up. The risk is speed: his top figure (62) is well below Souper Survivor's 73, so he needs a step forward or a favorite who doesn't fire.

Magical Trickster (4-1). Second choice. Biggest per-start in the playable group ($31,017 on one start) and the best figure (67) of the horses at a price. He beat Signature Winner on debut despite a bump at the break and a 6-wide run turning for home, which is a strong effort. Moran wins 21%, Carey 18%, together 24%. Two cautions: this barn is 0-for-16 with last-out winners and only 6% with 2-year-olds, and he stretches from 5 1/2 to 7 furlongs in his first stakes. Moran also leaves Signature Winner to ride him, which is a vote of confidence.

It Wasn't a Bluff (8-1). Flyer. Per-start only about $7,400 and still a maiden after four starts, but the trend is steadily up (Beyers in the mid-40s, then 56, then 59), and his last two came in stakes: 4th at 89-1 in the $300K Simcoe and 3rd in the Bull Page. He's proven on the synthetic (4 starts, two 3rds). Weak connections are the knock: Bynoe 6%, combo 5%. A fair price for a third-place type; a show saver more than a win play.

Slovan (20-1). Pass. Top Beyer 34, won a $25K maiden claimer at 1 1/16 miles last out, and now cuts back to 7f against stakes company. The barn has a nice 2-year-old ROI ($4.85) on a small sample, but the figures are nowhere close.

Bottom line

Signature Winner at 6-1 is the play: proven at the distance, hot barn, best combo ROI in the race, and his price is held up by a stablemate favorite. Magical Trickster at 4-1 is the second choice and the one to watch on the board (if he drifts toward 5-1 he's live). It Wasn't a Bluff at 8-1 is a small show saver at most. Check the tote at post: Signature Winner needs to stay at 7-2 or longer to remain on your list.
