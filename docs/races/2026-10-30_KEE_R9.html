Race recap

Result: Hughes won ($3.64, $2.58, $2.10), American History 2nd, Salahudin 3rd. The Grade 1 Breeders' Cup Juvenile, 1 1/16 miles on a fast dirt track in 1:42.27 (:23.36, :46.65, 1:10.86, 1:35.98). Exacta 5-6 paid $6.32 per $1 and the trifecta $8.65 per 50 cents. Gaudreau and Earth Song scratched.

What happened

The pace the write-up wanted never really formed. Damavand, not one of the three projected speed horses, made the lead along the inside, and the half went in :46.65, honest but not a meltdown. Hughes stalked two wide, took over on the far turn, inched clear near the eighth pole and widened late, a clean trip for the best horse. American History chased from between horses, was bumped near the quarter pole, swung five wide into the lane and needed a long drive to get 2nd. Salahudin was a bit keen early, was bumped at the quarter pole and again in the stretch, and missed the place. Coach Pope bid between horses and hung to 4th. He Is No Lie stumbled at the start, nearly lost the rider, and trailed.

Grading the handicap

The ranking held almost exactly: Hughes off the list as the horse to beat, then American History, Salahudin and Coach Pope in that order. The named risk for American History was the 15-point figure gap to Hughes, and that is what decided it. The board also did what the write-up warned about: American History went off at 3.92-1, just over the 3.5-1 line, so the bet still qualified. Salahudin at 4.67-1 qualified too. The flyer Damavand got the lead and gave way.

Takeaway

Both picks hit the board and the race still lost money, because a 1-1 favorite with a 100 Beyer won as he should. When the favorite's figure sits well above par and everyone else's sits well below, the place and show pools pay too little to cover a $6 WPS ticket. That is a race to pass or play win-only on a bigger price.

Pre-race handicap

The Grade 1 Breeders' Cup Juvenile for 2-year-olds, 1 1/16 miles on the dirt, purse $2M, Beyer par 97. Eight entered, six runners after scratches. Post 4:55 PM ET. Odds are morning line (DRF Formulator, 9 AM).

Off the list

Hughes (1-1) is the class of the race: 4-for-4, the only triple-digit figure in the field (100 winning the American Pharoah G1 at this distance), and Prat with Baffert (38% together). He is also no price, so he is the horse to beat and the reason the others pay.

Scratched: Gaudreau (8-1, Chad Brown) and Earth Song (20-1). Gaudreau beat Coach Pope in August, so his exit removes one live closer and tightens the field to six.

Pace

Three horses want the front on TimeformUS early pace: Coach Pope 113, He Is No Lie 112 (from post 8), and Hughes 106, with Salahudin 99 not far off. DRF's notes call the race light on pace, but those numbers say the early half should be honest, which is the setup a late runner needs.

Playable horses, ranked

1. American History (9-2) is the play. Best per-start money in the race ($230,607 on 2 starts, $461,213 total) and a rising line, 73 to 85, the 85 coming when he won the Breeders' Futurity G1 at this exact track and distance on Oct 3 after breaking slow, beating Coach Pope by a length. He owns the best late pace in the field (TimeformUS late 97, next best 76), and if the three speed horses press each other he is the one coming. Pletcher and Velazquez are 39% at Keeneland ($2.87 ROI), and the 4-furlong bullet here Oct 14 (best of 30) says he is fit. The risk: his 85 is 15 points below Hughes's 100, and if Hughes gets loose on an easy lead, there is nobody to run down.

2. Salahudin (6-1) is the improver. Unbeaten in three, figures climbing every start (58, 75, 83), and $130,724 per start. He beat He Is No Lie in the Iroquois G3 at a mile at Churchill after blowing the start and going wide, which is a better effort than the 83 reads. Baffert wins 29% (32% at 31 to 60 days) and the works are bullets (Oct 25 CD :47 best of 80). The catches: first try at 1 1/16, a third different rider in three starts, and he is Baffert's second string behind the favorite.

3. Coach Pope (4-1) has the top figure among the playable horses (88 in August) but it has slid since (88, 86, 83) and he already lost to American History at this trip. Ortiz with McPeek is strong (33% together), but McPeek is only 8% in graded stakes ($0.57), and he will be in the speed fight from post 7. Fair, flat, and under the bigger prices.

4. Damavand (20-1) is the flyer. Pletcher has won this race three of the last four years, the colt won his maiden at this track and distance Oct 4 in a duel after being bumped and carried wide, and his figures went 62 to 75 with a bullet work Oct 14. The 75 is far from par, so he needs a big jump, but at 20-1 he fits a small saver.

5. He Is No Lie (15-1) has speed and an honest second in the Iroquois (82), and the stumbled 52 at Del Mar is excused. Post 8 forces him to use himself early against Coach Pope and Hughes, and O'Neill is 6% in graded stakes. Pass.

Bottom line

American History at 9-2 is the bet: course-and-distance G1 winner, rising figures, best closing kick, and a pace setup that should help him. Salahudin at 6-1 is the second choice on the rising line and the Baffert barn. Damavand at 20-1 is a small flyer. Watch the board: American History could get bet under 7-2 as the clear second choice, and if he does the play shifts to Salahudin.
